# Correction 4: Differentiation from Existing Literature & Official Citations

## 1. Ready-to-Use Paragraph for the Final Report (English)

### Differentiation from Existing Literature and Contribution of This Study

The positive association between a local authority's socioeconomic standing and its aggregate academic performance in Israel is well documented in official government and policy publications. Specifically, the National Authority for Measurement and Evaluation in Education ([RAMA, 2018](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fmeyda.education.gov.il%2Ffiles%2FRama%2FTakzir_Meitzav_2018.pdf), [2019](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fmeyda.education.gov.il%2Ffiles%2FRama%2FAshkelon_Hesegim_Ravshnati_2019.pdf), [2024a](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frama.gov.il%2Freports%2Fses-achivement-gap), [2024b](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Frama.gov.il%2Freports%2Frama-2023-data)) regularly publishes national and multi-year municipal achievement reports demonstrating consistent score differentials across socioeconomic background tiers, and policy analyses by the Knesset Research and Information Center ([Knesset RIC, 2016](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Ffs.knesset.gov.il%2Fglobaldocs%2FMMM%2F8f1207dc-1277-e511-80d1-00155d0ad6b2%2F2_8f1207dc-1277-e511-80d1-00155d0ad6b2_11_8695.pdf); [Lerer, 2025](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Ffs.knesset.gov.il%2Fglobaldocs%2FMMM%2F442a717b-2eb3-f011-a865-005056aa9911%2F2_442a717b-2eb3-f011-a865-005056aa9911_11_21264.pdf)) as well as the Taub Center for Social Policy Studies in Israel ([Blass, 2020](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.taubcenter.org.il%2Fwp-content%2Fuploads%2F2020%2F12%2Fachievementsangapseng.pdf)) have extensively examined the structural role of the Central Bureau of Statistics ([CBS, 2024](https://www.google.com/search?q=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.cbs.gov.il%2Fhe%2Fmediarelease%2FDocLib%2F2024%2F230%2F24_24_230b.pdf)) socioeconomic index in shaping municipal resources and educational outcomes. Rather than merely reproducing this established baseline trend, our study focuses on **municipal deviation and outlier analysis**: we compare data-driven academic clusters (generated via unsupervised K-Means clustering) and supervised model predictions (Random Forest and Linear/Logistic models) against the official CBS socioeconomic clusters to identify which local authorities systematically outperform or underperform relative to their socioeconomic baseline, and which demographic, geographic, and subject-level characteristics distinguish these overperforming authorities.
